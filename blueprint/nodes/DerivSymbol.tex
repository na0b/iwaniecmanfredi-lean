\begin{definition}
A \emph{derivative symbol} is a triple $d=(i,j,\beta)$ consisting of two
non-negative integers $i$ and $j$ and a Boolean value $\beta$, subject to the
requirement
\[
  i+j>0 .
\]
The positive integer $|d|=i+j$ is called the \emph{order} of $d$.

A derivative symbol is the formal name of one of the factors $H^s$ in the
convention of Section~5 of the paper, which reads: ``Given a positive integer
$s$ let $H^s$ denote one of the partials
$\partial^sH/\partial\xi^i\partial\bar\xi^j$ or
$\overline{\partial^sH/\partial\xi^i\partial\bar\xi^j}$, where $i$ and $j$ run
over non-negative integers such that $i+j=s$.''  The symbol $(i,j,\beta)$
names the partial $\partial^{i+j}H/\partial\xi^i\partial\bar\xi^j$ when
$\beta$ is false and its complex conjugate when $\beta$ is true; its order
$i+j$ is the superscript $s$, and the requirement $i+j>0$ is the paper's
restriction to positive $s$.

The paper leaves the particular $i$, $j$ and the presence or absence of the
conjugation unspecified (``we do not specify which one of them''), recording
only the order $s$.  Here they are recorded as data.  This is a presentational
choice with no change of mathematical content: a statement about all
monomials of a given type is a statement about all choices of these data.
\end{definition}
