\begin{lemma}
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}$ be open, let $H\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$ be
$C^\infty$ on $\Omega$, let $s,k\ge0$ be integers and let
$P\in\mathbb{P}(s,k)$ relative to $H$ in the sense of \noderef{PClass}.  Then
$P$ is $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \noderef{PClass} fix an integer $r\ge0$, complex numbers $c_1,\dots,c_r$ and
lists $M_1,\dots,M_r$ of derivative symbols, each of type $(s,k)$, with
\begin{equation}\tag{1}
  P(\xi)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}c_\alpha H^{M_\alpha}(\xi)\qquad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathbb{C}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 1: every symbol value is $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$.}  Let
$d=(i,j,\beta)$ be a derivative symbol and put
$g_d=\partial^{i+j}H/\partial\xi^i\partial\bar\xi^j$, the iterated derivative
of \noderef{WirtingerIter}.  By part (a) of \noderef{WirtingerIterSwap},
$g_d$ is $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$.  By \noderef{SymbolValue}, $H^{d}$ is either
$g_d$ or its pointwise complex conjugate $\overline{g_d}$.  Complex
conjugation is an $\mathbb{R}$-linear isometry of $\mathbb{C}$, so composing a
$C^\infty$ function on $\Omega$ with it again yields a $C^\infty$ function on
$\Omega$.  Hence $H^{d}$ is $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$ in either case.

\emph{Step 2: every monomial is $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$.}  By
\noderef{MonomialValue}, $H^{M_\alpha}$ is the pointwise product of the symbol
values $H^{d}$ over the finitely many entries $d$ of $M_\alpha$, and is the
constant function $1$ when $M_\alpha$ is empty.  A finite pointwise product of
$C^\infty$ functions on $\Omega$ is $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$, and constants are
$C^\infty$; so $H^{M_\alpha}$ is $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$ for each
$\alpha=1,\dots,r$ by Step 1.

\emph{Step 3: conclusion.}  By (1), $P$ agrees at every point of $\mathbb{C}$,
hence in particular at every point of $\Omega$, with the function
$\xi\mapsto\sum_{\alpha}c_\alpha H^{M_\alpha}(\xi)$.  A finite
$\mathbb{C}$-linear combination of $C^\infty$ functions on $\Omega$ is
$C^\infty$ on $\Omega$, so that function is $C^\infty$ on $\Omega$ by Step 2,
and therefore so is $P$.
\end{proof}
